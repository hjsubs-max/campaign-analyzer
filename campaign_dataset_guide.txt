CAMPAIGN ANALYZER DEMONSTRATION DATA

Status: SYNTHETIC. All records and simulation choices were created for this
assignment. No real business data or advertising benchmarks were used.

FILES
campaign_data_synthetic.csv: load this into the Streamlit app.
campaign_dataset_generate_and_check.ipynb: optional regeneration and validation
companion. Python cells were executed in order in one fresh Python namespace
and outputs recorded; no Jupyter kernel was used for that run. It requires
Python 3, NumPy, and pandas. Rerunning writes the CSV in the working folder.

COVERAGE
4,040 rows; 24 campaigns; 183 calendar dates; April 1-September 30, 2026.
Channels: Facebook, Instagram, Pinterest, Twitter (the label used in the handout).
Audiences: Prospecting, Retargeting, Existing Customers.
Goals: Sales, Traffic, Awareness.
All money is USD. The CSV uses UTF-8, comma separators, one header row,
ISO dates, integer counts, and numeric money with two decimal places.

GRAIN AND KEYS
One row = one campaign on one day. Each campaign has one channel, audience,
and goal. Unique key: date + campaign_id + channel. Group campaigns by ID;
campaign_name is the display label. Absence outside a campaign's active dates
means the campaign was not active; it is not a missing historical partition.
Every day in the overall period has records.

COLUMNS
date: calendar reporting date, YYYY-MM-DD; no timestamp/timezone conversion.
campaign_id: stable unique campaign identifier, CMP001-CMP024.
campaign_name: searchable display name.
channel: advertising platform; map to platform if the app uses that name.
audience: target-audience segment.
goal: campaign objective; map to campaign_goal if the app uses that name.
impressions: ad impressions, integer >= 0.
clicks: ad clicks, integer >= 0 and <= impressions.
conversions: attributed PURCHASES, integer >= 0 and <= clicks, for all goals.
spend: reported advertising spend in USD, >= 0.
revenue: revenue attributed to those purchases in USD, >= 0; known on every row.
currency: USD on every row; do not mix currencies when aggregating.
dataset_type: synthetic on every row; retain the label in the demo.

SIMULATION ASSUMPTIONS
The fictional advertiser is a consumer retail store. Purchase values vary by
campaign and day. Conversions represent purchases even when the campaign goal
is Traffic or Awareness, so the conversion totals remain comparable. Purchases
are assigned to a single campaign; there is no cross-platform double counting
in this simulation. Revenue excludes tax, shipping, refunds, product cost,
agency fees, and operating costs. This is a clean simulation, not an ad-platform
export or evidence of real channel performance.

TIME-SERIES DEMONSTRATIONS
Daily noise and weekend variation are simulated. Summer Launch campaigns run
May 15-August 31. Discover the Brand starts April 15. Instagram Summer Launch
purchase probability increases after June 15. Pinterest Traffic purchase
probability increases after July 1. Twitter CPM increases and purchase
probability declines after August 15. These are chosen simulation rules;
observed charts will also include random variation and changes in campaign mix.

METRIC DEFINITIONS
Apply all filters first. Then sum raw measures before calculating:
CTR = SUM(clicks) / SUM(impressions).
CVR = SUM(conversions) / SUM(clicks); this is click-to-purchase conversion rate.
CPC = SUM(spend) / SUM(clicks).
CPA = SUM(spend) / SUM(conversions).
ROAS = SUM(revenue) / SUM(spend), displayed as a multiplier such as 2.3x.
ROI on an ad-spend basis = (SUM(revenue) - SUM(spend)) / SUM(spend).
Label the last metric "Ad-spend return" or "ROI (ad-spend basis)" and state that
product and operating costs are excluded. It is not full business profit ROI.
Display CTR, CVR, and ad-spend return as percentages: multiply fractions by 100.
If a denominator is zero, display N/A instead of infinity or an invented zero.
Do not average row-level or campaign-level rates to get portfolio totals.
For an empty selection, show a clear no-data state.
Missing revenue is not included in this file. In other uploads, never silently
treat missing revenue as zero or calculate complete-data ROAS without a warning.
